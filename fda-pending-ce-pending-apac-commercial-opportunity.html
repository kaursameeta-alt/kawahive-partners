FDA Pending or CE Pending? Could Your MedTech Team Miss an Alternative Route to Enhance Revenue?

A commercial decision framework for Series A–C MedTech founders and investors.

Your company is waiting for FDA clearance. Or it has FDA clearance but is still waiting for CE marking. Before treating the next milestone as the only path forward, could selected Asia-Pacific markets offer a commercial opportunity worth testing?

← Back to KawaHive Insights

Potential customers, distributors and competitors are not standing still while your team waits. The strategic question is whether waiting for the next regulatory milestone is creating an opportunity cost: could a selected APAC market help you validate a credible route to customer demand and revenue sooner?

That is a hypothesis to test, not a conclusion to assume. KawaHive works on MedTech commercialisation, not regulatory submissions. The purpose here is to prompt a better market-entry decision—not to turn this into a regulatory guide or imply that any country guarantees faster sales.

Regulatory eligibility is not market access. Market access is not customer demand. And customer interest is not a purchase order.

The case for exploring APAC is strongest when the available foreign evidence may support a defined local route, the remaining steps fit the company’s runway, and there is a credible buyer and procurement path to investigate. That does not prove demand or revenue. It means waiting may not be the only strategic option worth considering.

Two scenarios. One commercial question.

Scenario A — CE marked, FDA clearance pending

If the product already has a qualifying CE certificate, should the company wait for FDA clearance before investigating every other commercial opportunity?

Singapore and Malaysia publish conditional routes that may recognise specified EU Notified Body evidence for eligible devices. Product class, intended use, IVD category and exact supporting documents matter; a CE mark alone is not a regional passport. Local registration and other obligations still apply. [1][2]

Commercial implication: identify whether one specific market has a reachable buyer and a credible procurement path before assuming the next US milestone must come first.

Scenario B — FDA cleared, CE marking pending

If the product already has a qualifying FDA decision, could the company investigate a selected APAC opportunity while its European pathway continues?

Singapore and Malaysia also describe conditional routes for specified US FDA evidence. The relevant FDA status—such as 510(k), De Novo or PMA—matters, and local registration is still a separate decision. FDA clearance is not a universal right to sell across APAC. [1][2]

Commercial implication: test whether a named buyer, local route and support model make the opportunity worth pursuing; do not forecast revenue from the FDA decision alone.

India and Sri Lanka have their own product-specific rules and decision processes. Nepal and Myanmar should not be assumed to offer a general foreign-approval shortcut without direct confirmation of the current local requirements. [3]–[6]

Five questions before you commit more runway

  1. Who has the problem—and who can buy? Name the first target accounts, likely clinical users, budget owner and procurement stakeholders. Three discovery conversations can help expose assumptions; they are not proof of a whole market.
  2. What makes a purchase possible? Understand the buying process, budget timing, required clinical or operational evidence, tender rules and who can authorise the next step.
  3. Can the channel generate repeat demand? Assess the distributor’s access, incentives, sales capacity, installation and service capability—not just its size, opening order or request for exclusivity.
  4. What could stop interest becoming an order? Test pricing, reimbursement or funding, incumbent alternatives, procurement barriers, training, support and local service assumptions.
  5. What evidence justifies further investment? Set a clear next milestone—such as a qualified buyer process, a lawful evaluation, a first purchase order or repeat order—and define what you must learn before spending more.
The decision test

Can we validate a specific buyer and a credible procurement path while keeping the cost, legal requirements and distraction within our runway?

What can you validate while you wait?

Start with commercial discovery, not a premature launch plan. A team can identify target hospitals and laboratories, interview potential buyers, understand procurement calendars, test the distributor’s ability to reach the right accounts, map decision-makers and learn what evidence purchasing committees will need.

Before any product demonstration, import, promotion, paid evaluation or clinical research, separately confirm what the country permits for the specific device and activity. Special permissions are limited and are not substitutes for ordinary registration or a sales pathway. [7]–[11]

The objective is to produce useful commercial evidence without implying that demand has already been proven—or compromising the company’s core US or European plan.

Make the decision against the runway

Before committing to an APAC expansion, document:

  • the named buyer, budget owner and procurement route;
  • the local approval and other requirements that apply to the exact product and intended use;
  • the evidence clinicians and purchasing committees will require;
  • the partner’s incentives and ability to install, train, service and support the product;
  • the local price, competitive alternative and cost to serve; and
  • the next observable commercial milestone and the evidence needed to reach it.

If you cannot identify a buyer, a credible procurement route and a support model, a potentially relevant registration pathway is not enough to justify expansion. Compare the cost of waiting with the cost of pursuing a market that may not convert.

Conclusion: is waiting creating an opportunity cost?

The evidence does not establish that APAC is a general bridge while FDA clearance or CE marking is pending. It supports a narrower strategic question: is waiting causing your team to overlook a specific, testable commercial opportunity?

Selected APAC markets may be worth investigating when the exact foreign evidence fits a defined local route, the remaining requirements fit the runway, and there is independent evidence of a reachable buyer and procurement path. On the official material reviewed, Singapore and Malaysia provide the clearest conditional reasons to investigate both scenarios. India and Sri Lanka may be relevant for particular products under their own rules. None of these findings establishes customer demand or revenue.

The question for your next board or commercial review:

“While we wait for our next regulatory milestone, is there a specific APAC customer opportunity worth validating—and can we test it without putting our core plan at risk?”

That is a commercial decision, not a regulatory shortcut.
